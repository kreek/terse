# Structures by document type

An outline picks a shape before it picks sections. Match the shape to
the document type, then adapt it to the material. A shape the material
resists is the wrong shape.

- **Report or memo**: the pyramid. The answer first, then about three
  supporting arguments, each with its evidence.
- **Argumentative essay**: thesis, reasons with evidence, the strongest
  counterargument and its rebuttal, conclusion.
- **Technical explainer**: what it is, why it matters, how it works,
  how to use it, edge cases.
- **PRD**: problem, goals and non-goals, users, requirements, success
  metrics.
- **Design doc**: context and problem, proposed solution, alternatives
  considered, trade-offs, rollout.
- **Blog post**: hook, promise, body sections, takeaway.
- **Narrative feature**: an opening scene, then the "so what"
  paragraph, then sections that weave theme with time, then the
  resolution.
- **News or announcement**: the inverted pyramid. The most newsworthy
  facts first, so a reader who stops after one sentence still has the
  story.

Every shape keeps the same spine. The governing thought lands early.
Each section earns its place. The piece ends on the ask or the
resolution, not a summary.
